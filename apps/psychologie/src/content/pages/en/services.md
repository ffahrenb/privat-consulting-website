## Stress & Recovery

Stress is a natural response to demands. It becomes problematic when the strain consistently exceeds one's resources and recovery fails to happen. This affects not only well-being, but also performance, relationships and long-term health.

My master's thesis at the University of Vienna examined the mechanisms of stress and recovery and the role of personality factors in this process. The thesis was supervised by Prof. Urs Nater, one of the leading stress researchers in the German-speaking world. This scientific foundation underpins my counselling in this area.

**Typical concerns:**

- Persistent work overload and lack of recovery
- Stress from role conflicts, high expectations or changes at work
- The feeling of losing the overview
- Prevention: understanding and strengthening your own approach to stress
- Recovery: developing effective recuperation strategies and integrating them into daily life

**My approach:**
I work evidence-based, drawing on current insights from stress research. The central question is how to restore or strengthen the balance between demands and recovery. Together, we analyse your situation, identify stress factors and develop concrete strategies for relief.

**Format:** Individual sessions, 60 minutes, online via Zoom.

## Vocation coaching

Are you facing a professional reorientation? Are you asking yourself what you really want – and what you need to feel well and lead a meaningful life?

Vocation coaching is a structured, solution- and future-oriented process. In approximately 8 sessions, we work together on your vocation – what drives you at your core – and develop from it a powerful and concrete vision for your next step. The concept of vocation is deliberately kept broad and is independent of professional background, age or worldview.

**The method**

Methodologically, the process builds on systemic coaching and extends it with elements from learning theory and knowledge management. The underlying method was developed at the Department of Knowledge Management at the Vienna University of Economics and Business and has been successfully applied and scientifically evaluated for over 15 years (Theory Wave). At its core is a structured learning process that combines learning from the past with learning from an envisioned future scenario.

**The process: approximately 8 sessions to your personal vision**

The process follows the three-step approach **Discover – Strengthen – Implement** and works along three core questions:

1. **What do I need?** – Which needs are important to me so that I feel well and can lead a meaningful life? This is about substantive needs – not superficial wishes, but what you need at your core.
2. **What do I really want?** – What draws me when I look to the future? What are my aspirations and dreams? This is where the draft of your personal vision emerges.
3. **What can I do?** – What skills, resources and experiences do I bring? How can I develop my talents and align them with my vision?

At the end of the process, you'll have a formulated vision – detailed, powerful and compelling – along with a concrete plan for implementation.

**Who is vocation coaching suitable for?**

- People facing a career change
- Completing school or university and the question: what's next?
- Re-entering the workforce after a break
- Retirement and the question of a new chapter
- Anyone who wants to clarify and sharpen their life vision

**My background:**
I'm a trained systemic coach specialising in knowledge-based vision development (training with Prof. Alexander Kaiser, WU Vienna). At WU Vienna, I spent several years researching needs in social systems, vision development and organisational learning, and published 16 peer-reviewed papers on these topics. This research forms the scientific foundation of my coaching work.

More on the scientific foundations: [Science & Publications](/en/science/)

**Format:** Individual sessions, 60 minutes, online via Zoom. Approx. 8 sessions, typically at weekly or biweekly intervals.

## Mountain psychology

Mountains challenge the whole person – physically, technically and mentally. Anyone who climbs, goes ski touring or moves in alpine terrain knows situations where the mind becomes the decisive factor. Wolfgang Güllich put it simply: "The brain is the most important muscle in climbing."

As a psychologist and active mountain athlete, I bring both perspectives together. I'm an SAC mountain guide, ski instructor and climbing instructor, and I volunteer as a climbing instructor for SAC Section UTO in Zurich. In Vienna, I founded and led a climbing group for socioeconomically disadvantaged young people over three years. I know the situations you're describing – not just from theory, but from my own experience on rock, ice and in alpine terrain.

**Typical concerns:**

- **Fear of falling when climbing** – Fear of falling is one of the most common mental challenges in climbing – and according to research, the performance-limiting factor for more than half of all climbers. It's normal and serves an important protective function. But when it dominates your climbing and blocks your progress, it's worth taking a closer look. The point is not to climb without fear – but to learn to manage it.

- **Mental blocks** – Situations where you know you can do it – but your mind won't cooperate. Whether on a climbing route, on a ridge or on a steep slope: mental blocks can be understood and addressed.

- **Risk assessment** – Good decisions in the mountains require not only technical knowledge but also psychological competence. How do I assess risks realistically? How do I handle group pressure? When do I say no?

- **Mental strength** – Focus, calm and confidence in exposed moments. I support you in building mental strategies you can draw on in the mountains.

**My approach:**
I work with proven methods from sport psychology, adapted to the mountain context:

- **Graded exposure** – Gradually approaching the anxiety-triggering situation. Not jumping in, but systematically working your way forward – from top-rope through short lead falls to the falling limit. Out of the comfort zone, but not into the panic zone.
- **Visualisation and mental training** – Mentally climbing the route, building positive movement imagery, using focus techniques like the "box method": seeing only the next metre, blocking out everything else.
- **Breathing regulation** – Consciously controlling breathing in stressful situations. Breathing is one of the few levers through which we can directly influence the autonomic nervous system.
- **Cognitive restructuring** – Recognising and changing negative thought patterns. From "I must not fall" to "I trust my ability and my safety system."
- **Activation regulation** – Finding the optimal arousal level for the given climbing situation. A dyno requires a different level of activation than a technical slab.

Sport psychology research shows: fall training without mental preparation and support often produces only short-term results. Lasting change comes from combining mental training with practical exercise.

**My background:**
- SAC mountain guide
- Ski instructor
- Climbing instructor
- Volunteer climbing instructor, SAC Section UTO Zurich (since 2023)
- Founded and led a climbing group for disadvantaged young people, Vienna (2012–2015)
- Longstanding personal experience on rock, ice and in alpine terrain

**Format:** Individual sessions, 60 minutes, online via Zoom. Outdoor formats on request.

## Coaching for leaders & professionals

Leadership has become more complex in an increasingly digitalised world. Leaders and professionals face the challenge of exerting influence, making decisions under uncertainty and keeping the overview – often without formal authority and with conflicting demands.

Through my own experience as a Product Owner in IT, I know the challenges of technology-driven roles first-hand. I know what it feels like to translate between business and IT, to mediate between strategy and execution, and to stay effective under pressure.

**Typical concerns:**

- **Role clarification** – What is my mandate, what's expected of me, and which of those expectations do I actually want to accept? Especially in new or cross-functional roles, this clarification is crucial.

- **Decision-making** – In important professional decisions, the problem is often not a lack of options but a lack of clarity. Together, we organise your thoughts, identify relevant criteria and develop a basis for a well-founded decision.

- **Dealing with pressure and complexity** – High expectations, conflicting demands and constant change are everyday reality for many leaders. I support you in keeping a clear head and acting effectively – even under pressure.

- **Personal development** – Coaching offers a protected space for honest reflection. What drives me? Where am I getting in my own way? What do I want to change?

**My approach:**
I work solution-oriented, resource-focused and with a psychologically grounded set of methods. My goal is to strengthen your ability to find your own answers and to act with autonomy.

**Format:** Individual sessions, 60 minutes, online via Zoom.
