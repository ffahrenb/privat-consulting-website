## Data-driven organisational development

Many organisations invest heavily in data strategies, analytics platforms and governance structures. The technology gets built, roles get defined, policies get written. And yet, not much changes. Data stewards don't live their roles, business units see no value, the data team feels isolated. Dashboards exist, but nobody makes decisions based on the data.

The problem is rarely technical. It's organisational and psychological.

I know this dynamic from my own experience. As Product Owner Data & Analytics, I built a company-wide data governance programme – from the operating model through roles and responsibilities to the data community. I know what it feels like when a stewardship model works on paper but doesn't land in the organisation. And I know what it takes to make it work.

### Topics I address

**Building a data culture** – Data culture doesn't emerge from a manifesto. It emerges when people understand why data matters, when roles are clear and when there are spaces where data-driven work becomes tangible. I help organisations shape this process deliberately.

**Bringing data governance to life** – You've defined roles, created a glossary and set up a governance board – but it remains a formality? I work with you on the question of how governance becomes something people experience as meaningful and actively support.

**Bridging business and data teams** – The biggest hurdle in many data projects isn't the technology, it's the communication. Business units and data teams speak different languages, have different expectations and see different value. I facilitate this interface and create shared understanding.

**Clarifying needs** – My research at WU Vienna focused on how to systematically identify and prioritise needs in social systems. I apply this knowledge to make relevant needs visible in change processes – before they surface as resistance.

### My approach

I'm not an external consultant who presents frameworks and leaves. My approach combines the insider view of a data practitioner with the analytical distance of an organisational psychologist. I work with you in the process – not above it.

**Formats:** Consulting, facilitation, workshops, ongoing support over several months.

## Change management in digital transformation

Digital transformation is not an IT project. It changes roles, responsibilities, ways of working and power dynamics. When organisations introduce new systems, automate processes or want to make better use of data, the demands are not just technical – they're fundamentally human.

In my work as a Product Owner, I re-mapped core processes end-to-end, clarified responsibilities and modelled enterprise architecture. I know what it's like when a new system goes live and half the workforce doesn't understand why. And I know that the answer is rarely better training – it's a better process of involvement and clarification.

### Topics I address

**Change support for system implementations** – ERP, CRM, data warehouse, BI tools: every system implementation changes how people work. I support the human side of this change – from communication strategy through handling resistance to post-go-live support.

**Clarifying roles and responsibilities** – Digital transformation shifts accountabilities. Who decides on data quality? Who owns a process end-to-end? Who has which permissions in the new system? I help clarify these questions – not just on paper, but in daily practice.

**Leadership in change processes** – In digital transformations, leaders are often both affected and driving the change. I coach leaders to consciously navigate this dual role and guide their teams through the transition.

**Understanding and using resistance** – Resistance is not a disruption, it's information. It reveals where needs haven't been heard, fears haven't been addressed or participation hasn't been enabled. I help you use resistance as a resource rather than fighting it.

### My approach

I combine psychological expertise with a concrete understanding of the technical and organisational reality. I speak both the language of IT and the language of the business. This enables me to work at the intersection where most change projects get stuck.

**Formats:** Consulting, facilitation, workshops, coaching of project leads.

## Enterprise architecture & process landscape

Enterprise architecture creates transparency about the interplay of business processes, data, applications and IT infrastructure. In my hands-on work, I've treated EA not as a theoretical model but as a practical tool for strategic decisions.

### Topics I address

**EA modelling and introduction** – Modelling business processes, data objects, applications, interfaces and integrations, and strategically linking them to the IT roadmap. I work with standards like ArchiMate and tools like BOC Adoit.

**Re-mapping the process landscape** – Analysing core processes end-to-end, clarifying responsibilities and KPIs, linking processes with data and analytics. Not as a one-off project, but as a foundation for continuous improvement.

**Business-IT alignment** – Bridging business requirements and IT architecture. Putting project portfolio and roadmap decisions on a sound footing.

**EA governance** – Introducing EA standards, decision records and a regular governance cadence.

### My approach

Enterprise architecture is valuable when it's understood and used by the people in the organisation. I bring the technical modelling competence – and the understanding of how to anchor EA in an organisation.

**Formats:** Consulting, modelling, workshops.

Leaders in data-adjacent roles – CDO, Head of Data, Product Owner, digitalisation project lead – face particular challenges: exerting influence without formal authority, translating between business and IT, between strategy and execution, between urgency and diligence.

### Typical concerns

- Dealing with resistance, uncertainty and complexity
- Making decisions under pressure
- Balancing operational demands with strategic leadership
- Reflecting on one's own leadership approach

### My approach
